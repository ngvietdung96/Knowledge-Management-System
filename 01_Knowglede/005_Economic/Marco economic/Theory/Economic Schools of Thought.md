## Monetarism
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monetarism

Monetarism in the US and the UK:
	In 1979, United States President [Jimmy Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Carter "Jimmy Carter") appointed as Federal Reserve Chief [Paul Volcker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Volcker "Paul Volcker"), who made fighting inflation his primary objective, and who restricted the money supply (in accordance with the [Friedman rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedman_rule "Friedman rule")) to tame inflation in the economy. The result was a major rise in interest rates, not only in the United States; but worldwide. The "Volcker shock" continued from 1979 to the summer of 1982, decreasing inflation and increasing unemployment.
	In May 1979, [Margaret Thatcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Thatcher "Margaret Thatcher"), Leader of the [Conservative Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_\(UK\) "Conservative Party (UK)") in the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom "United Kingdom"), won the [general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_United_Kingdom_general_election "1979 United Kingdom general election"), defeating the sitting [Labour Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_\(UK\) "Labour Party (UK)") led by [James Callaghan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Callaghan "James Callaghan"). By that time, the UK had endured several years of severe [inflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflation "Inflation"), which was rarely below the 10% mark and stood at 10.3% by the time of the election. Thatcher implemented monetarism as the weapon in her battle against inflation, and succeeded at reducing it to 4.6% by 1983. However, [unemployment in the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unemployment_in_the_United_Kingdom "Unemployment in the United Kingdom") increased from 5.7% in 1979 to 12.2% in 1983, reaching 13.0% in 1982; starting with the first quarter of 1980, the UK economy contracted in terms of real gross domestic product for six straight quarters.
